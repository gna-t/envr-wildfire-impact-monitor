    Method Notes

    1. City search uses Open-Meteo Geocoding, constrained to U.S. locations. Coordinates become the incident reference point.
    2. Air quality uses Open-Meteo current and hourly variables: U.S. AQI, PM2.5, PM10, ozone, and nitrogen dioxide.
    3. NASA FIRMS Area API is queried for recent detections (last 1 day) inside a bounding box around the selected city.
    4. Distances to detections are calculated with haversine geometry and summarized as closest hotspot and activity level.

    Transport Limits

    • Hotspot detections do not independently confirm smoke exposure in the searched city.
    • Smoke transport depends on wind fields, boundary-layer dynamics, and topographic effects.
    • Air quality fields may be modeled/interpolated and differ from local regulatory monitor values.
    • This tool is educational screening support, not an official risk or health advisory product.

    Environmental Insight

    Wildfire plumes often travel beyond fire perimeters, so urban air quality can shift even when active burns are outside city boundaries. PM2.5 is especially important because these fine particles can penetrate deep into the respiratory system and are linked to acute and chronic health impacts. This briefing format helps compare regional fire activity with local pollution signals in a transparent screening workflow.
